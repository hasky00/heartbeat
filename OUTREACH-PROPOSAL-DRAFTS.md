## Common Concept Note

### HEARTBEAT Gaza: professionally validating a ten-home family shelter pilot

HEARTBEAT Gaza is an independent, early-stage concept initiated by Hasky. It
explores a simple, durable and affordable family-home model for displaced
families in Gaza, with dignity and essential infrastructure at the centre.

The proposed first pilot is intentionally limited to ten approximately 30 m2
family homes. Each concept home includes two sleeping rooms, a private bathroom,
a basic kitchenette and provision for weather protection, clean water,
sanitation and basic energy. The current material is conceptual and has not
been approved for construction.

The first objective is professional validation, not fundraising or immediate
construction. HEARTBEAT is seeking qualified organizations to test the need,
scope, engineering assumptions, WASH approach, safeguarding requirements,
procurement model, access constraints and preliminary cost range.

Hasky can contribute concept development, design coordination, public
documentation, technology and transparent reporting. Hasky is not an NGO,
charity, fiscal sponsor or Gaza implementation organization.

Hasky will not receive, hold, route, manage, approve or account for donations,
construction funds or beneficiary funds. Any future funding must be received
and controlled exclusively by an approved fiscal host or humanitarian partner,
which would also control procurement, contractors, banking, audit and donor
reporting.

The project is currently asking potential partners for one or more of the
following:

- technical and humanitarian review;
- fiscal hosting and compliant fund administration;
- a qualified Gaza implementation pathway;
- a conditional contribution toward defined pilot costs;
- suitable materials, equipment, professional services or logistics; or
- an introduction to an appropriate partner or funder.

No recipient is described publicly as a partner, supporter or funder without a
written agreement and permission.

## Humanitarian Discovery Questions

### Short opening

> HEARTBEAT Gaza is an independent concept, not an approved programme. I am
> looking for informed criticism before deciding whether it should progress.
> Hasky will not receive or manage humanitarian funds. May I ask three brief
> questions about relevance, validation and the correct partnership route?

### Three questions for a first email

1. **Relevance:** Does a small, durable family-home pilot address a meaningful
   shelter gap in the present Gaza context, or would another shelter approach
   be more appropriate?
2. **Validation:** What evidence, technical reviews and safeguarding conditions
   would your organization require before considering a proposal like this
   credible?
3. **Correct route:** Which team, local partner or external organization would
   be the appropriate place for a concept-stage discussion?

Do not place more than three questions in the first email. The remaining
questions are for a call or follow-up requested by the recipient.

### Need and programme fit

- How do organizations currently distinguish emergency, transitional and
  durable shelter needs in Gaza?
- Is a ten-home pilot useful for learning, or too small to justify the
  operational burden?
- Which families or circumstances should the concept be designed around,
  without attempting to select beneficiaries ourselves?
- What common assumptions made by outside shelter concepts are unrealistic?

### Technical and humanitarian validation

- Which shelter, WASH, protection, accessibility and fire-safety standards
  should govern an initial feasibility review?
- What must be validated locally before materials, dimensions or systems can be
  specified?
- What evidence would be required concerning land, tenure, permissions,
  unexploded ordnance, drainage, utilities and environmental risks?
- Which parts of the current concept should be removed, simplified or tested
  first?

### Community participation and safeguarding

- How should affected families participate in design decisions from the
  beginning?
- What safeguarding and complaints mechanisms would be mandatory?
- How can the project document progress without exploiting images, stories or
  personal information from affected families?
- What local leadership must exist before the project could be legitimate?

### Implementation and responsibility

- What type of registered organization would need to own and implement a pilot?
- Which responsibilities must remain with the humanitarian partner rather than
  Hasky?
- What access, procurement, sanctions, anti-diversion and contractor checks
  would be required?
- What would cause a responsible organization to stop or postpone the project?

### Funding and private-sector participation

- At what stage is it responsible to approach private companies for
  conditional funding or in-kind contributions?
- Which costs could companies appropriately support: validation, materials,
  WASH, energy, logistics, professional services or monitoring?
- What information must be included in a credible preliminary budget?
- Can your organization receive and control restricted corporate support, or
  would a separate fiscal host be required?

### Closing question

> Based on what you have heard, what is the single most useful next step, and
> may I send a two-page concept note for private review?

### Framing to avoid

Avoid questions that seek premature validation, such as:

- "Do you think this is a great idea?"
- "Will you partner with us?"
- "How much will you donate?"
- "Can we use your name on the website?"
- "Can you help us build immediately?"

Replace them with questions about relevance, evidence, risks, ownership and the
correct review process.

## Draft 1: Open Collective Europe Foundation

**Subject:** Eligibility inquiry: EU-based initiative with proposed humanitarian
work in Gaza

Dear Open Collective Europe Foundation team,

I am the initiator of HEARTBEAT Gaza, an independent early-stage concept
coordinated from Denmark for a small family shelter pilot for displaced
families in Gaza.

The proposed first phase is ten approximately 30 m2 family homes focused on
weather protection, clean water, private sanitation, basic energy and dignity.
The project is still at concept stage. No fundraising or construction has
begun, and all designs require professional humanitarian, engineering and WASH
validation.

The concept aligns with a humanitarian public-benefit purpose by addressing
safe family shelter, water, sanitation, basic energy and dignity. Its proposed
beneficiaries are outside the EU, while the initiative and its project
coordination are based in Europe. Before preparing a full application, I would
like to clarify whether that geography is eligible under your current
humanitarian focus and European hosting strategy.

The indicative programme would proceed in conditional phases:

1. months 0-2: fiscal-host and humanitarian-partner eligibility;
2. months 2-4: shelter, engineering, WASH and safeguarding review;
3. months 4-6: local feasibility, access, site and cost validation;
4. months 6-9: partner-led fundraising and procurement planning; and
5. months 9-12: a ten-home pilot only if all approvals and conditions are met.

No funding is currently committed. Prospective sources would include
conditional corporate contributions, restricted foundation grants, donated
materials or services, and public contributions made only through the approved
host. We are preparing private inquiries to relevant water, energy, materials,
logistics and strategic-funding organizations. We would not describe any source
as confirmed until a written commitment exists.

Hasky would never receive or manage project funds. Any contributions would
remain under the fiscal host's control and be used only through approved
budgets, documentation and implementation arrangements.

Would your team confirm whether an EU-coordinated initiative proposing
humanitarian activity in Gaza is currently eligible for hosting? If so, I can
provide the attached concept note and develop the full proposal, timeline and
funding plan required for assessment.

Kind regards,

## Review Checklist Before Any Letter Is Sent

- [ ] Confirm the recipient's current official contact channel.
- [ ] Address a named person when a verified name is available.
- [ ] Select only the relevant request; remove unrelated options.
- [ ] Attach or link only the approved concept note.
- [ ] Confirm all dimensions and claims against the public website.
- [ ] Do not include bank details, wallets or payment instructions.
- [ ] Do not attach personal identity or beneficiary information.
- [ ] Send privately from the official project email.
- [ ] Record the date and response.
- [ ] Do not publish the recipient's name as a partner without permission.
